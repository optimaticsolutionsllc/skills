How can a computer find the right John clip?

Rose needs one short video for a LinkedIn post. There are thousands of recordings. She knows the idea she wants. She does not know which words John used.

An embedding is a list of numbers made from words. The numbers let the computer find ideas that are alike.

What IS an embedding?

Pretend each idea gets a little number card. A computer can compare two number cards much faster than it can watch thousands of videos.

Tap a sentence

Watch the numbers move:

We use 4 easy-to-read numbers here so you can see the idea. The real model that powered Letz Go used 512 numbers.

The most important part

The numbers are made by a model.

The model is a program that learned patterns from lots of text during training. You give it words. It gives back numbers.

A real embedding's 512 numbers are NOT neat labels like “time,” “sales,” or “care.” We use those easy labels only in our pretend practice model.

A number card is not a video. It is not a quote. It is not the right answer. It is just a way to compare pieces of text.

Can an embedding be wrong?

Yes. A close match only means that two pieces of text look related. It does NOT mean one is right, safe, or something John actually said.

Two practice clips
Clip 1

“Stop posting daily if it steals time and brings no clients.”

Clip 2

“Post every day, even if it steals your free time.”

They mention the same things: posts, days, and time. But they give opposite advice.

Your turn

Should Rose use either clip before checking the video?

Correct. The numbers help Rose find clips. Rose checks what John really said, who he was talking about, what happened before and after, and whether the post keeps the same meaning. This is important when one recording contains two client stories.

Why does this matter to you two?

You already have a workflow that can use embeddings. You do not need to train a new AI model to start.

🎬 Rose finds the proof

She writes “John on working less.” Search brings back likely transcript moments. She opens the source, checks the story, and picks the right clip for the post.

🔧 Tim builds the system

He can connect recordings, transcripts, Google Drive, and a clip list. The system makes number cards, keeps links, and returns the right source when asked.

📈 John keeps his voice

Posts can use his real advice, his real client stories, and the right recording. The system helps people find proof; it should not invent proof.

The full loop, in plain words

John speaks→Words are written down→Words get numbers→Rose searches→The numbers are compared→A clip is found→A human checks

The embedding does one job: it helps find things that mean something similar.

Three things you should remember

1. Embedding = numbers made from text.

2. Similar numbers can help find similar ideas.

3. The original source must stay attached, so a person can check it.

What to ask Claude Code to build next

Not a real AI model yet. First, make sure your clips are ready to search.

  1. Collect each transcript.
  2. Save short sections with video links and time.
  3. Make and save an embedding for each section.
  4. Search by meaning and show the original section.
  5. Let Rose mark: right clip / wrong clip.
